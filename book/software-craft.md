# What is a Software Crafter?

I’m glad you asked. The software‑craft movement was established around 2012 and quickly gained popularity in the UK and Germany. Today there are thousands involved in the creation of software who embrace the ideas of this movement.

As Software Crafters, we view our profession as something that can be taught—much like the craft of a carpenter. We develop skills that can be learned and refined with better tools. This contrasts sharply with the academic view of computer science, which often treats programming as a unique gift rather than a teachable skill. I reject the notion that software is merely an art reserved for a select few “special” people. While some talent and interest are certainly helpful—especially an inclination toward structure and logic—these qualities can be cultivated in anyone. The rest comes down to experience, and everything can be taught.

Around this idea—that our craft is a lifelong journey and that we can always learn more—a community has formed. Its members are eager to share experiences, learn new things, and discuss ideas. One of the most notable gatherings for us are the SoCraTes conferences. You can find a list of them online on [softwarecrafters.org](https://www.softwarecrafters.org/). These are non‑profit, community‑run open spaces, ranging from one‑day events to multi‑day workshops.

I invite you to join our community! We welcome everybody, regardless of profession, experience, skin color, gender, or other attributes because we believe that only diversity will help us to find the best solutions to the big and small problems we are lucky to be facing every day.

I hope to see you at one of the OpenSpaces!
