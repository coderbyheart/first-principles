# Books to read

This is a selection of books I highly recommend to read.

## REST in Practice: Hypermedia and Systems Architecture

by Jim Webber, Savas Parastatidis, Ian Robinson

<https://amzn.to/4pjwKCT>

It has been more than 10 years, since I first read REST in Practice and it greatly influenced the way I design web application backends. It gave me a universal toolkit (HTTP) to build decoupled microservice architectures, and I am convinced that I even today many of the learnings I got from reading the book for the first time.

For this reason I also highly recommend this book (amongst others) as a must read in my talks and to whomever asks for a book recommendation on software architecture.

Overall, the sentiment of the book still holds very true today. We need to build resilient, decoupled systems that can handle unpredictable load patterns. The book does a very good job of showing how all of this can be achieved with a ubiquitous communication protocol: HTTP. This principle is truly language- and platform-neutral which makes it so universally applicable.

I have given it another review it from the point of view of a team that develops software using serverless technologies, in TypeScript on Node.js and provided a reading guide that helps to navigate some of the outdated sections:

<https://coderbyheart.com/rest-in-practice-reading-guide>

## The Lean Mindset: Ask the Right Questions

by Mary and Tom Poppendieck

<https://amzn.to/4qExDHr>

This book is only 160 pages long, but it packs a punch: companies that follow the agile way of building software driven by customer needs will eventually deliver a good result. But good does not mean exciting nor innovative. Just good does not set you apart from any other software company out there and makes you replaceable as a service provider.

In their book Mary and Tom take the reader on a quest that eventually leads to finding the answer for the most powerful question: how can organizations create breakthrough innovations in a predictable (or at least sustainable) way?

Chapter by chapter they lay out their understanding of the fundamentals that are needed to build software that truly serves the customer, and they don't all short on highlighting what's needed to achieve that: engaged employees with a deep understanding of the underlying business rules, a clear purpose and vision for the product they are building. Every section is supported by a real-world case study so you don't have to take their word for granted, but can see the respective theories in action in real life.

This book easily made it to the top of my reading recommendation for managers, founders, and developers as well. Keep a copy of the book handy, so you can check once in a while their questions to ponder provided after every chapter.

## Accelerate: Building and Scaling High-Performing Technology Organizations

by Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble, Gene Kim

<https://amzn.to/3LufpJG>

I don't think this book needs an introduction, it is absolutely a must read for anyone interested in building great engineering organizations.

## All Hands on Tech

by Matthias Patzak & Sophie Seiwald-Højer

<https://amzn.to/4q0dRWV>

All Hands on Tech comes along as a thin paper-back, but actually delivers a punch. Written by two seasoned tech execs with many years experience in German enterprises, they have compiled a timeless compendium that explains in a very well structured approach how to build excellent tech organizations. Every chapter starts with the juxtaposition of the typical (bad) status quo that we have all experienced in corporate environments and explains the blueprint for the ideal in one or two pages. The rest of the chapter is spent in the detailed explanation on how to implement the change colored with some real-world examples from the authors' own experiences. Each chapter closes with a recommended reading list, which will leader you further into the respective topic.
This structure makes the book useful as a reference for many years, you can pick a topic that you struggle with and use All Hands on Tech as the starting point to dive deep into the solution space.
There are very technical chapters and I especially enjoyed the very well reasoned advice to kill staging environments and how tech teams should be aligned for flow. It's great to see how the authors lined up these chapters to lead into the cultural aspect of high performing teams which the book closes with.
I believe this book to be a very helpful implementation guide for the findings detailed in Accelerate, and if you haven't read that one, start with it first.

## Architecture for Flow: Adaptive Systems with Domain-Driven Design, Wardley Mapping, and Team Topologies

by Susanne Kaiser

<https://amzn.to/3MWqB2j>

Without unnecessary fluff it delivers a blueprint for building software-delivery organisations that are ready to scale and grow. Susanne manages to bind together three powerful concepts: wardley maps, team topologies, and domain driven design in a way that makes it applicable both for managers and for engineers, who are all needed to deliver customer value in a world of constant changes (hashtag#vuca). She even provides a roadmap for turning a legacy organisation with a software monolith around. Overall this book provides tons of concrete advice that is valuable for decades. A book like this rare, don't miss out on it!
